# The desk routine · making the website tags worth something

Every enquiry that comes from the website opens with one line, for example:

    [Source: Website | Door: Bridal | CTA: Check my date | Campaign: organic]

Plain English: this person came from the website, through the bridal door,
pressed the check-my-date button and no paid campaign sent them.

The tag says where somebody CAME FROM. It cannot say whether they booked or
paid. That part is a person at the desk, and it takes under a minute a day.

## The three steps

1. **When an enquiry lands:** keep the bracket line. Do not delete the message.
   If the desk keeps a day book, copy the Door word (Salon, Bridal, Concept,
   General) into it with the date.
2. **When it becomes a booking:** mark that enquiry BOOKED in the day book, on
   the same row.
3. **At month end:** count the rows per door: how many enquiries, how many
   booked. Two numbers per door. That is the whole report.

## What the numbers can honestly say

- Which door brings enquiries and which door brings bookings.
- Whether a campaign name (the Campaign part of the tag) pulled anyone.

## What they cannot say

- What anybody spent. The till knows that, the tag does not.
- Anything about walk-ins, calls or people who found the salon on Google Maps
  without touching the website.

If the counting stops, the tags become decoration. The website cannot do this
part for you.
